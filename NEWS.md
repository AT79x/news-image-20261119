## 1. SHENZHEN SUMMIT — Trump, Xi and Putin meet three-way (hero story)
- US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian
  President Vladimir Putin meet in Shenzhen on Thursday 19 Nov on the
  sidelines of the APEC summit — the first ever trilateral meeting of the
  leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers. One hour is allocated
  for the three-way meeting, after the APEC plenary session.
- Agenda: nuclear arms control (pushed by Trump, resisted by Xi), Ukraine and
  Iran. Trump also holds a bilateral meeting and working lunch with Xi, and a
  one-on-one with Putin. No major breakthrough is expected; the symbolism is
  the story. Trump arrived in China on Wednesday; Xi greeted him at the
  airport in Shenzhen.

## 2. GULF WAR WIDENS — Iran hits US bases, tankers burn in Hormuz

## 3. GAZA — Israeli tanks cross the Yellow Line
- Israeli tanks advanced west of the Yellow Line in central Gaza on
  Wednesday, rolling onto the main Salah al-Din road near the Bureij and
  Nuseirat refugee camps — the first ground operation beyond the line since
  the October 2025 ceasefire. Families fled as the operation went on into
  Thursday. Three Palestinians were killed in Israeli strikes on Wednesday.
- Israel calls it a precise operation against Hamas infrastructure; Hamas
  calls it a flagrant ceasefire violation. The line marks the boundary of the
  two-thirds of Gaza still under Israeli control.

## 5. EU LENDS UKRAINE 45 BILLION EUROS
- The European Commission proposed on Wednesday a new 45 billion euro
  ($49.45 billion) loan to Ukraine for 2027 — 20 billion euros in budget
  support and 25 billion in military aid — covering about two-thirds of
  Ukraine's 70 billion euro funding gap for next year.
- It is a "limited-recourse" loan: Ukraine repays the principal only after
  receiving war reparations from Russia. It needs approval from all 27 EU
  member states; four states (Slovakia, Hungary, Czech Republic, Bulgaria)
  signalled they will opt out.
- Sources:

## 6. THE HAGUE — world court rules on Rohingya genocide today
- The International Court of Justice delivers its judgment on Thursday
  19 Nov at 10:00 local time at the Peace Palace in The Hague, in the case
  The Gambia brought against Myanmar in November 2019 over the 2017 campaign
  against the Rohingya, which drove more than 700,000 people to Bangladesh.
- The court will say whether Myanmar as a state committed genocide under the
  1948 Genocide Convention. In 80 years the court has never found a state
  responsible for genocide. The judgment is final, with no appeal. (Outcome
  was still pending when this entry was built; the image states only that the
  court rules today, not the verdict.)
